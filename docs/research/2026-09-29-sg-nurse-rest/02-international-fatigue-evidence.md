# International rest rules and fatigue evidence for nurse rosters

Date: 2026-09-29. Scope: international law, professional guidance and research. Singapore law is in a separate report. None of the sources below binds a Singapore hospital. They show what other systems treat as the safe floor.

## Short answer

- Most sources that set a number put rest after a run of nights at about two days. JNA asks only 24 h after a single night. That means 46 to 48 hours from the end of the last night. It does not mean one calendar day off.
- The common cap on consecutive nights is 2 to 4. Injury risk climbs by the 4th night.
- An 11-hour minimum gap between shifts is law in the EU and Taiwan. Quick returns (under 11 h) have a systematic review, a longitudinal accident study and a randomised trial against them.
- Forward rotation (AM, then PM, then Night) is standard guidance. The trial evidence behind it is weak.
- Shifts of 12 h or more link to more errors and worse safety ratings. The evidence is observational.

## 1. Law and binding instruments

| Source | Rule | Quote |
|---|---|---|
| EU Working Time Directive 2003/88/EC, Art. 3 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32003L0088)) | 11 h daily rest | "a minimum daily rest period of 11 consecutive hours per 24-hour period" |
| Same, Art. 5 | Weekly rest 24 h + 11 h | "per each seven-day period ... a minimum uninterrupted rest period of 24 hours plus the 11 hours' daily rest" |
| Same, Art. 8 | Night work length | "normal hours of work for night workers do not exceed an average of eight hours in any 24-hour period" |
| Same, Art. 6 | 48 h week | "the average working time for each seven-day period, including overtime, does not exceed 48 hours" |
| Same, Art. 17(3)(c)(i) and 17(4)(a) | Hospitals and shift changes can derogate from Arts. 3, 5 and 8, but only with compensatory rest | "care provided by hospitals or similar establishments" and "each time the worker changes shift and cannot take daily and/or weekly rest periods" |
| ILO Nursing Personnel Recommendation R157 (1977), para. 38(2) ([text via Barbados Parliament copy of NORMLEX](https://www.barbadosparliament.com/uploads/sittings/attachments/79f0e3a77901ae319ac820667bed88ff.pdf)) | 12 h between shifts for nurses | "Nursing personnel assigned to shift work should have a period of continuous rest of at least 12 hours between shifts." |
| ILO R157 para. 36(2) | Weekly rest at least 36 h | "The weekly rest of nursing personnel should in no case be less than 36 uninterrupted hours." |
| ILO R157 para. 33(2) | 12 h working day cap | "The working day, including overtime, should not exceed 12 hours." |
| Australia, Nurses Award 2020, cl. 13.4 ([Fair Work](https://awards.fairwork.gov.au/MA000034.html)) | 10 h between shifts, 8 h by agreement | "a rest break of 10 hours between the completion of one ordinary work period or shift and the commencement of another" |
| Same, cl. 13.1(f) | Days off | "free from duty for not less than: (i) 2 full days in each week; or (ii) 4 full days in each fortnight; or (iii) 8 full days in each 28-day cycle" |
| Same, cl. 13.1(d) | Shift length | "a maximum of 10 hours exclusive of meal breaks" |
| Taiwan Labor Standards Act, Art. 34 ([MOJ law database](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=N0030001)) | 11 h between rotating shifts, 8 h only by ministry notice | "a rest period of at least eleven hours continually" |

Notes:
- ILO R157 is a Recommendation. It is not binding, even for states that ratify Convention C149.
- The Nurses Award 2020 has no specific rest-after-nights clause. I checked the Fair Work text of clauses 13 and 20. An earlier search summary cited "clause 23" for the 10 h break. The current award numbers it 13.4.
- Taiwan: a 2021 hospital scheduling study says the 11 h rule now applies to nurses, so "the hospital is no longer allowed to schedule workers using an 8-hour interval" ([ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0957417421005443)). I found no Ministry of Health and Welfare notice granting hospitals the 8 h exception. Unconfirmed. Taiwanese researchers also report a medical-sector rule of "at least 24 h off between night and day shifts" (Chang 2017, below). I did not find the source rule.

## 2. Professional guidance

**UK HSE, HSG256 *Managing shift work*** ([PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg256.pdf)):
- "When switching from day to night shifts or vice versa, allow workers a minimum of 2 nights' full sleep."
- "Limit 12-hour night shifts to 2-3 consecutive nights."
- "In general, limit consecutive working days to a maximum of 5-7 days"
- "Where possible, adopt a forward-rotating schedule for rotating shifts rather than a backward-rotating schedule."
- "Limit shifts to a maximum of 12 hours (including overtime)"

**UK NHS nurses.** NHS Employers states staff "have the right to 11 hours of uninterrupted rest between shifts and a day off each week" ([Shift work essentials](https://www.nhsemployers.org/articles/shift-work-essentials-nhs-staff)). The RCN notes that the HSE two-nights rule "is not law" ([RCN](https://www.rcn.org.uk/Get-Help/RCN-advice/working-time-rest-breaks-on-call-and-night-work)). I found no NHS-wide rule giving nurses a fixed rest period after nights. Local trust policies vary.

**UK resident doctors (comparison).** The 2016 contract caps runs at four nights. After the 2019 review, 46 h rest follows any run. The BMA factsheet says: "you should not work more than four shifts where at least three hours are between 11pm and 6am. After this type of shift, you must be rostered for a minimum of 46 hours' rest" ([BMA factsheet 2024](https://www.bma.org.uk/media/ujjppmvv/bma-contractual-rights-factsheet-junior-doctors-web-revised-september-2024.pdf)). NHS Employers FAQ, original 2016 wording: "maximum of four consecutive night shifts with minimum 46 hours rest after a run of either three or four" ([NHS Employers FAQ](https://www.nhsemployers.org/publications/faqs-2016-doctors-training-contract)). The same contract sets a maximum of seven consecutive shifts, then 48 h rest.

**ANA 2014 position statement** ([PDF](https://www.nursingworld.org/globalassets/practiceandpolicy/health-and-safety/nurse-fatigue-position-statement-final.pdf)):
- "Plan one or two full days off of rest to follow five consecutive 8-hour shifts or four 10-hour shifts. Consider two rest days after three consecutive 12-hour shifts"
- "Keep consecutive night shifts to a minimum for rotating shifts."
- "Establish at least 10 consecutive hours per day of protected time off duty"
- "Limit work weeks to 40 hours or fewer per week." "Limit shifts to 12 hours or fewer."
- A newer ANA statement now supersedes it and keeps the 40 h/week figure ([ANA page](https://www.nursingworld.org/practice-policy/nursing-excellence/official-position-statements/id/addressing-nurse-fatigue-to-promote-safety-and-health/)).

**Japanese Nursing Association, Guidelines on Night Shift and Shift Work for Nurses (2013)** ([JNA News Release Vol. 11](https://www.nurse.or.jp/english/assets/pdf/2013nr_11.pdf)). This is the most specific nursing guidance found:
- "Provide 11 hours or longer time between shifts."
- "Up to 2 consecutive night shifts."
- "Night shifts should basically be within 8 times a month in a system of 3 shifts per day."
- Consecutive working days: "Up to 5 days."
- "Provide a rest period of 48 hours or more after 2 consecutive night shifts. A rest period of 24 hours or more is desirable after 1 night shift."
- "The roster to be forward rotating."
- "Avoid starting the morning shift before 7 AM."

**ICN.** I found no ICN position statement with numeric limits on hours or nights. I searched icn.ch and its position statements page. The ICN Code of Ethics (2021) asks national associations to develop guidelines on "fatigue, safety" ([ICN Code](https://www.icn.ch/sites/default/files/2023-06/ICN_Code-of-Ethics_EN_Web.pdf)).

**Hong Kong Hospital Authority.** I found no public HA rule on rest after nights for nurses. A 2000 paper on the HA rostering system lists example ward rules such as "Prefer a day off right after a night shift" and "Should not assign three night shifts in a row" ([Chun, IAAI 2000](https://cdn.aaai.org/IAAI/2000/IAAI00-003.pdf)). These are old examples, not HA policy. Unconfirmed as current practice.

**Malaysia.** I found no Ministry of Health nursing shift guideline with rest-after-nights numbers. I checked the MOH Nursing Division "Acts & Guidelines" page listing and Employment Act 1955 summaries. A secondary summary claims an 11 h rest between workdays. I did not find that rule in the Act. MOH nurses are civil servants under service circulars I did not find. Unconfirmed.

## 3. Research evidence

**Recovery after a night block**
- Two 12 h nights: "full recovery needs at least three days off work". Most recovery came on day 1 ("Fatigue improved 77% on RD 1, 23% on RD 2"). Austrian nurses, diary study ([Haluza 2019, J Nurs Manag](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jonm.12664)). Single study.
- Taiwan: "One day off appeared to be insufficient to adapt back to a daytime shift after two NSs." ([Ergonomics 2017, PubMed 28498029](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28498029/)). A follow-up found "one off-duty day was insufficient to recover back to a daytime shift after four consecutive NSs" ([Chang 2017, Human Factors](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720817711285)). Small studies.
- Extending the break after nights from 31 h to 55 h lowered exhaustion and distress in 30 nurses, with no change in objective markers ([PMC9691089](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9691089/)). Small non-randomised trial.

**Consecutive nights and injury risk**
- Meta-analysis ("Risk Index" update, 29 studies): highest risk "when shift length exceeds 11 h, and when work takes place during the night (particularly for >3 consecutive night shifts)" ([Fischer 2017, PubMed 29064297](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29064297/)). Summaries report a 36% higher risk on the 4th night ([SafetyInsights](https://safetyinsights.org/2022/03/03/updating-the-risk-index-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis-of-occupational-injuries-and-work-schedule-characteristics/)). Meta-analysis, cross-industry.
- HSE's fatigue index report notes errors rise over four consecutive nights ([HSE RR446](https://humanfactors101.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/the-development-of-a-fatigue-risk-index-for-shiftworkers-rr446.pdf)).

**Quick returns (under 11 h)**
- Systematic review: quick returns link to short sleep, fatigue and sleepiness ([Vedaa 2016, Ergonomics, PubMed 26072668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26072668/)).
- 1,692 Norwegian nurses over two years: more quick returns raised the risk of "causing harm to patients/others (IRR 8.49, 95% CI 2.79-25.87)". Fewer quick returns cut it ([Vedaa 2020, SJWEH](https://www.sjweh.fi/show_abstract.php?abstract_id=3906)). Longitudinal cohort.
- Cluster RCT, 66 hospital units, 811 staff: "half the number of short daily rest periods had a positive effect on sickness-related absence days and spells" ([Djupedal 2025, JAMA Netw Open, PubMed 40952742](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40952742/)). Randomised trial.
- Scoping review: "quick returns between shifts appeared to increase pathologic fatigue" ([Dall'Ora 2016, IJNS, PubMed 27045561](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27045561)).

**Forward vs backward rotation**
- Systematic review: "support for the use of forward rotating shift systems in preference to backward rotating shift systems, at last as far as 8-h shifts are concerned" ([Driscoll 2007, Sleep Med Rev, PubMed 17418596](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17418596)).
- Cochrane 2023: only "very low-certainty evidence that forward rotation reduced sleepiness" ([Hulsegge 2023, Cochrane](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD010639.pub2/full)). The main harm of backward rotation is the short gap it creates (PM then AM). Rule R1 below already blocks that gap.

**12-hour shifts and patient safety**
- Rogers 2004, 393 US nurses, 5,317 shifts: error risk rose when "shifts lasted more than twelve hours ... or when they worked more than forty hours per week". Odds ratio 3.29 for shifts of 12.5 h or more ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.23.4.202), [PubMed 15318582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15318582/)). Diary cohort.
- Griffiths 2014, 31,627 nurses in 12 European countries: shifts of 12 h or more linked to worse ratings of care quality and patient safety and more care left undone ([Medical Care, PubMed 25226543](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25226543/)). Cross-sectional, self-report.
- Clendon and Gibbons 2015 systematic review: 13 studies. Six found more errors on shifts over 12 h, four found more on shifts up to 8 h, three found no difference ([IJNS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0020748915000735)). Mixed evidence.
- Stimpfel 2012: shifts of 10 h or more meant up to 2.5 times the odds of burnout and intent to leave ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2011.1377)). Cross-sectional.
- Dall'Ora 2019: 601,282 shifts. Long shifts link to more sickness absence ([J Nurs Manag, PubMed 29978584](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29978584/)). Longitudinal, routine data.
- Trinkoff 2011: pneumonia deaths more likely where nurses reported long hours (OR 1.42) and "lack of time away from work (OR = 1.24)" ([Nursing Research](https://journals.lww.com/nursingresearchonline/abstract/2011/01000/nurses__work_schedule_characteristics,_nurse.1.aspx)). Cross-sectional.

## Implications for the roster rules

These rules rest on international references. The Singapore report decides which of them are legal duties in Singapore. HARD means the solver never breaks the rule. STRONG means a heavy penalty. The solver breaks a STRONG rule only if coverage is impossible otherwise, and it flags each break to the planner. Hours count from actual shift end to next shift start. They do not count calendar days.

| # | Rule | Label | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | At least 11 h between the end of one shift and the start of the next. This bans any PM then next-day AM pair with a gap under 11 h. | HARD | Law in the EU (Art. 3) and Taiwan (Art. 34). ILO R157 asks 12 h. RCT (Djupedal 2025), cohort (Vedaa 2020), systematic review (Vedaa 2016). Note: the EU lets hospitals derogate with compensatory rest. |
| R2 | After any run of nights (including a single night), no AM or PM shift starts until at least 46 h after the last night ends. In practice this means the post-night sleep day plus one full calendar day off. The post-night day alone is not a rest day. | HARD | Contract rule for UK doctors (46 h). Expert guidance: JNA (48 h after 2 nights), HSE (2 nights' full sleep), ANA (2 days after 3×12 h). Small studies show 1 day is not enough (Chang 2017, Haluza 2019). Not statute for nurses in any source found. |
| R3 | Prefer 48 h or more after 3 or more nights. Prefer a third day off after 12 h nights. | STRONG | Single diary study (Haluza 2019). Small trial (31 h vs 55 h). |
| R4 | Maximum 4 consecutive nights. | HARD | Contract rule for UK doctors. Meta-analysis shows risk rises by the 4th night (Fischer 2017). |
| R5 | Prefer at most 3 consecutive nights. Prefer 2 where staffing allows. | STRONG | Expert guidance: HSE (2 to 3 for 12 h nights), ANA (3×12 h), JNA (2). |
| R6 | No backward rotation inside a work run. The allowed order is AM, then PM, then N. If the gap is under 11 h, R1 already blocks PM to AM. N to AM or PM is already blocked by R2. | STRONG | Systematic review (Driscoll 2007). Cochrane found very low certainty. Guidance: HSE, JNA. |
| R7 | At least one rest period of 35 h (24 h + 11 h) in every 7 days. | HARD | Law in the EU (Art. 5). ILO R157 asks 36 h. NHS nurses get a day off each week. |
| R8 | At least 8 full days off per 28-day period, consecutive where possible. | STRONG | Law in Australia (Nurses Award cl. 13.1(f)). Matches the 28-day period. |
| R9 | Maximum 5 consecutive working days. Allow up to 7 only as an exception. | STRONG (5) and HARD (7) | Guidance: JNA (5), HSE (5 to 7), ANA (1 to 2 days off after 5×8 h). UK doctors' contract caps at 7. |
| R10 | No shift longer than 12 h, including planned overtime. | HARD | ILO R157 (12 h day). Guidance: HSE, ANA. Cohort and cross-sectional studies (Rogers 2004, Griffiths 2014). |
| R11 | Average rostered hours at most 48 per week over the period. Target 40 or fewer. | HARD (48) and STRONG (40) | Law in the EU (Art. 6). Guidance: ANA (40). Cohort (Rogers 2004: over 40 h raises errors). |
| R12 | At most 8 nights per nurse per 28 days on a 3-shift roster. | STRONG | Expert guidance (JNA). |

Open points for the Singapore report and the product decision:
- R1 depends on local shift times. If PM ends at 22:00 and AM starts at 07:00, the gap is 9 h, so R1 bans that pair.
- R2 needs a clear definition of the post-night day in the data model. A Night starting on day d ends on day d+1, so day d+1 is not free time.
- EU Art. 8 caps night work at an average of 8 h. Singapore Night shifts often run longer. That is a legal question for the Singapore report, not a solver default.
