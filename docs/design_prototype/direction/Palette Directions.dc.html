Turn 2 · Refining Clinical Mint

Four ways to take 1c further

Clinical Mint is now live in the app, so these four all start from it and vary one decision each: where the warmth lives (2a ink, 2c accent), how cool it goes (2b), and how much the canvas commits to colour (2d). Every option holds AA on both its card and its panel band.

2a Mint, Warm Ink

The mint canvas you picked, but the ink turns warm — espresso-brown text and slightly warm hairlines against the cool surface. Keeps 1c’s brightness while putting the warmth back in the type, where it reads as richness rather than dinge.

2b Saline

Pushes past mint into cool clinical blue-teal — the most obviously “hospital software” of the set, and the brightest. Highest legibility on a dense grid; the least warm option here, so treat it as the far edge of the range.

2c Mint & Honey

Cool canvas, warm accent — amber carries the brand and every primary action, so the warmth lands on the things you click. Trade-off: teal is freed up, but amber then sits close to the AM shift chip and the warning colour, so those need re-tuning.

The confident version: canvas deepens to a real sage green so white cards read as genuinely raised paper, not as slightly-brighter background. More colour commitment than 1c, and the strongest card separation of any option.

Turn 1 · Palette directions

Brighter, cleaner, still warm

Today’s canvas is #ede5dc — a fairly saturated sand. It reads dim because the background is doing the warmth work, so every white card floats on a yellow field. Each direction below moves the warmth somewhere else — into the accent, the ink, or just the hairlines — and lets the canvas come up toward white. Same layout, same content, five palettes. Reference an id in chat to go deeper.

1a Cloud Dancer

Pantone’s 2026 colour of the year — a creamy off-white that is neither stark nor beige. The safest move: same teal, same structure, canvas lifted ten steps and desaturated. Cards go pure white so they read as raised paper.

1b Porcelain & Clay

Warmth moves out of the canvas and into the accent. Near-neutral porcelain background, and a soft terracotta carries the brand instead of teal. The most visible change of the five — worth seeing before deciding how much warmth you actually want.

1c Clinical Mint

2026’s “medical-grade greens” read as clean without reading as cold. The canvas takes the faintest mint cast, the brand brightens, and the ink goes green-slate. Brightest of the five, and the most on-domain for a hospital tool — the trade is that the warmth is now cool-leaning.

1d Oat & Espresso

Keeps the most warmth of the five, but fixes the “dirty” problem by brightening the canvas to oat and pushing the warmth into the ink — deep espresso brown instead of near-black. Reads rich rather than dim. Closest in spirit to today's app.

1e Paper

The high-key extreme. Canvas almost white, cards separated by hairlines rather than fills, and the only warmth left is in the ink and the rules. Maximum clarity for dense roster grids — the risk is that a 28-day table starts to feel like a spreadsheet.
